# XYRA Principia Discipline

**Project:** XYRA_LOGIC_LAB
**Program Node:** LOGIC-FND-001
**Authority:** Human Committee / CBA
**Adopted:** 2026-09-05
**Status:** FOUNDATIONAL / GOVERNING

## 1. Purpose

The XYRA Principia Discipline establishes the foundational reasoning doctrine
for the modern XYRA Logic Backbone.

It is inspired by the foundational ambition of Alfred North Whitehead and
Bertrand Russell in Principia Mathematica: important formal conclusions should
be derivable from explicit foundations rather than accepted merely because they
appear obvious.

XYRA does not reproduce Principia Mathematica's notation or historical
architecture.

XYRA adopts the underlying discipline:

- make the foundation explicit;
- make the definitions explicit;
- make the transformations explicit;
- make the derivation inspectable;
- make the result independently checkable.

## 2. XYRA-FND-INVARIANT-001

Every formal conclusion shall be distinguishable from:

1. its premises;
2. its definitions;
3. its axioms or foundational assumptions;
4. its inference rules;
5. its assumptions;
6. its evidence support;
7. its derivation path.

No system component may collapse these categories merely for convenience.

## 3. Formal Validity Is Not Factual Truth

A valid derivation establishes that a conclusion follows under a stated formal
system from stated premises.

It does not establish that the premises are factually true.

XYRA shall preserve these separate questions:

EVIDENCE:
Is P supported?

LOGIC:
Given P and Q, does R follow?

VERIFICATION:
Does output O faithfully represent the evidence, model, or formal result?

DECISION:
What should a Human decide or do?

## 4. Proof Transparency

Where appropriate, formal results should support machine-readable derivation
information sufficient for:

- replay;
- dependency inspection;
- independent verification;
- identification of the rule used at each step;
- counterexample or countermodel analysis where applicable.

Future AI systems may propose reasoning.

They shall not substitute uninspectable model confidence for formal derivation.

## 5. Type Discipline

XYRA shall investigate formal type systems capable of preventing invalid
category transitions.

Candidate future categories include:

- OBSERVATION
- SOURCE_CLAIM
- SUPPORTED_CLAIM
- ASSUMPTION
- FORMAL_PREMISE
- DERIVED_CLAIM
- HYPOTHESIS
- PREDICTION
- VALUE_JUDGMENT
- RECOMMENDATION
- DECISION

These are research candidates, not yet implemented runtime types.

## 6. Declared Formal Context

Future formal outputs should identify, where applicable:

LOGIC
FOUNDATION OR AXIOM SET
PREMISES
ASSUMPTIONS
INFERENCE RULES
DERIVATION
RESULT
LIMITATIONS

The statement "R follows" shall mean:

"R follows within the explicitly declared formal system and assumptions."

It shall not silently mean:

"R is true in the world."

## 7. XYRA-FND-CERT-001

A future foundational certification target shall require the Logic Backbone to
derive elementary arithmetic from declared foundations.

The canonical certification theorem is:

1 + 1 = 2

The importance of this theorem is not computational difficulty.

The purpose is to demonstrate that XYRA can expose why the result follows from
its declared foundation rather than relying only on host-language arithmetic.

## 8. Machine Learning Relationship

Future machine-learning systems may:

- recognize patterns;
- propose formalizations;
- generate hypotheses;
- suggest inference paths;
- identify candidate counterexamples;
- estimate probabilities.

Machine learning shall not automatically become the source of formal authority.

Evidence grounding, formal verification, representation verification, and Human
authority remain distinct.

## 9. Human Authority

Formal reasoning may constrain, expose, challenge, and verify reasoning.

It does not replace Human authority.

AI proposes. Humans decide.
